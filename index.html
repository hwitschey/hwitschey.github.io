Project Overview

About This Project

This practicum project was conducted within a university accessibility and disability services office to assess employee burnout, organizational strain, morale, and workplace support needs — and to deliver concrete, actionable recommendations to leadership.

How It Was Done

Mixed-Methods Approach

The assessment was designed as an applied consulting project using a sequential mixed-methods design — moving from exploratory qualitative inquiry to quantitative measurement and back to rich qualitative contextualization.

🔍
Phase 1 — Exploratory Focus Group
Conducted an initial focus group (n = 6) to identify key themes, emerging concerns, and organization-specific context to inform survey instrument design.
Qualitative
📊
Phase 2 — Anonymous Employee Survey
Administered an anonymous survey (N = 16, 76% response rate) using validated burnout constructs alongside organization-specific items to measure emotional exhaustion, cynicism, personal accomplishment, workload strain, and turnover intentions.
Quantitative
🗣️
Phase 3 — Semi-Structured Interviews
Conducted individual interviews with 16 staff members (89% of full-time staff) to explore lived experiences of burnout, coping strategies, and perceived organizational supports and barriers.
Qualitative
📝
Phase 4 — Observational Data & Analysis
Supplemented interview and survey data with qualitative observations collected as an embedded member of the organization. Data were synthesized into a final consulting report with prioritized recommendations.
Mixed Analysis

What Was Found

Key Findings

Burnout was present across the department, though its severity and form varied by role. Employees in student-facing positions — particularly Disability Specialists — reported the highest levels of emotional exhaustion and cynicism.

3.86
Mean Emotional Exhaustion
On a 0–6 frequency scale, with strong reliability (α = .946). Highest individual item: "I feel used up at the end of the workday" (M = 4.38).
3.78
Mean Cynicism Score
Measured via 7-item scale (α = .965). Emotional detachment and reduced patience were among the most commonly reported experiences.
5.69
Technology Burden — Highest Stressor
The single highest-rated item across the entire dataset: "Technology systems slow my work down" — indicating administrative tools are a primary driver of strain.
r = .60+
Burnout → Turnover Intent
Statistically significant positive correlation between burnout indicators and turnover intentions, indicating retention risk is meaningfully tied to burnout levels.
6.62
Colleague Support — A Protective Factor
Mean peer support rating of 6.62 / 6 scale. Strong colleague relationships emerged as the primary buffer against burnout across all roles and units.
5.13
Leadership Support — Room to Grow
Perceived leadership support (M = 5.13) was notably lower than peer support, indicating an opportunity for improvement in organizational communication and clarity.

Qualitative Themes from Interviews

  • Administrative and technology burden displacing meaningful student-facing work
  • Ambiguity in role expectations and shifting organizational priorities
  • Emotional labor demands tied to disability enforcement and student advocacy tensions
  • Strong sense of mission and student impact as a core source of resilience
  • Desire for more transparency, communication, and predictability from leadership
  • Hybrid flexibility identified as a significant contributor to employee wellbeing

What Was Recommended

Organizational Recommendations

Five evidence-based recommendations were delivered to leadership, each grounded in both the quantitative data and qualitative themes gathered across all phases of the assessment.

01
Reduce Administrative & Technology Burden
Prioritize review and streamlining of technology workflows that emerged as the highest-rated stressor across the department. Even incremental improvements to system efficiency would meaningfully reduce daily strain.
02
Protect and Maintain Hybrid Flexibility
Hybrid work arrangements were widely cited as a major contributor to employee wellbeing and work-life balance. Any changes to remote work policy should be approached carefully given its protective role.
03
Improve Leadership Communication & Clarity
Staff expressed a need for more consistent, transparent communication from leadership — particularly around role expectations, organizational decisions, and future planning. Regular structured updates would reduce ambiguity-related stress.
04
Invest in Role-Specific Burnout Prevention
Disability Specialists reported the highest burnout levels. Targeted support — such as reduced caseloads, dedicated supervision structures, or peer consultation — could buffer the most at-risk employees.
05
Build on Existing Peer Support Culture
Colleague support was the strongest protective factor identified. Leadership should actively cultivate and protect the informal peer networks and team culture that employees already rely on as a buffer against burnout.
