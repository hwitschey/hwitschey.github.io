Project Context

About This Engagement

As an embedded internal consultant, I independently led a full organizational diagnostic within a high-volume university accessibility and disability services unit — a team of up to 21 staff that had seen 93% growth in student demand since 2021, while simultaneously navigating staffing shortages, an office relocation, and evolving telework policy.

🎯
Consulting Objectives
Assess the scope and drivers of employee burnout; identify protective organizational factors; and deliver evidence-based recommendations to leadership focused on retention, workload sustainability, communication clarity, and organizational effectiveness.
🏢
Organizational Context
The unit served ~4,000 registered students — up from ~2,000 since 2021 (+93%). During the assessment, staff were navigating concurrent stressors: active staffing shortages, a building relocation, evolving telework expectations, and increasing service complexity.
📐
Theoretical Frameworks
Grounded in the Maslach Burnout Inventory–Human Services Survey (MBI-HSS), Job Demands-Resources Model (Bakker & Demerouti, 2007), and Emotional Labor Theory (Hochschild, 1983). Quantitative analysis in SPSS v29.
🔒
Confidentiality & Ethics
Organization anonymized for this portfolio. All data collected anonymously with aggregate reporting throughout to protect confidentiality in a small department where unit sizes ranged from 1–9 staff. Positionality documented and managed throughout.

How It Was Done

Mixed-Methods Approach

A sequential mixed-methods design — from exploratory qualitative inquiry through quantitative measurement, integrated via thematic coding and SPSS-based statistical analysis.

01
Focus Group
Exploratory session with 6 Disability Specialists to surface key concerns and inform instrument design. All 6 confirmed experiencing burnout.
Qualitative
02
Survey (N=16)
Anonymous Qualtrics survey distributed to all 21 eligible staff (76% response). MBI-HSS constructs + org-specific items. Analyzed in SPSS v29: descriptives, reliability (α), correlations, unit comparisons.
Quantitative
03
Interviews (N=16)
Semi-structured interviews with 16 of 18 full-time staff (89%). Custom codebook developed and applied. Themes compared to survey data for cross-method convergence.
Qualitative
04
Synthesis
Mixed-methods integration of SPSS output, coded interview themes, observational data, and archival review into a final report with feasibility-weighted recommendations.

What Was Recommended

Strategic Recommendations

Five evidence-based recommendations prioritized by data frequency and organizational feasibility. The three priority recommendations each included phased implementation plans and ready-to-use tools delivered alongside the report.

01
Reduce Cognitive & Administrative Burden Through Shared Resources
Administrative burden was the most consistently cited burnout driver. Primary intervention: a Communication Library — a governance-managed internal site with leadership-reviewed language templates for difficult student interactions. Delivered with a 5-phase implementation plan (structure design, content collection, peer review, leadership approval, ongoing maintenance). Directly targets the top-rated stressors: technology burden, time pressure, and repetitive emotional labor.
Priority 1 · 5-Phase Implementation Plan Delivered
02
Maintain Hybrid Flexibility During Organizational Stabilization
Hybrid work was the most consistently endorsed protective factor — functioning as an organizational resource reducing cognitive fragmentation and supporting recovery, not merely a preference. With 50% of staff in elevated turnover risk during active organizational transitions, recommended deferring any telework policy changes until staffing, facilities, and operations stabilize — and exploring feasible flexibility options even for historically in-person units.
Priority 2 · Retention Risk Implications
03
Improve Organizational Clarity, Communication & Priority Alignment
Inconsistent decision-making (73% of interviews) and lack of transparency (67%) were high-frequency themes. Delivered three tools: a Decision Change Log for tracking procedural updates and rationale, a Leadership Update Template for structured regular communications, and a centralized procedures repository recommendation. All low-cost, no-budget interventions reducing ambiguity-driven burnout.
Priority 3 · Three Tools Delivered
04
Invest in Role-Specific Support for High-Risk Positions
Disability Specialists showed substantially higher burnout than other units (EE: 4.64 vs. 2.80–2.90 elsewhere), with 67% in elevated turnover risk. Recommended targeted supports: structured peer consultation (“Collaboration Hours”), clearer role scope boundaries, and supervisory structures designed to address decision fatigue from high-volume, ambiguous gatekeeping responsibilities.
Additional Recommendation
05
Protect and Build On the Existing Peer Support Culture
Colleague support was the highest-rated item in the entire dataset (M=6.60/7) and the most frequently coded protective factor (87% of interviews). The 1.53-point gap between colleague (6.60) and leadership support (5.07) indicates peer networks are buffering what formal support structures are not providing. Leadership should intentionally protect these informal networks and avoid inadvertently disrupting them through policy or structural changes.
